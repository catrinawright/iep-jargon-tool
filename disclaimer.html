4 Laws Vary by State and District

Special education law in the United States is governed at both the federal and state levels. While IDEA establishes a federal floor of rights and protections, individual states may provide additional rights, different procedural timelines, or more expansive protections for students with disabilities. School districts may also have their own policies and procedures.

This tool is written primarily with reference to federal law and, where indicated, Illinois state law. References to specific timelines, procedures, or rights may not accurately reflect the law in your state or the policies of your school district. Examples include but are not limited to:

  • The age at which transition planning must begin (federal minimum is age 16; some states require earlier)
  • Procedural timelines for evaluations and IEP meetings
  • State-specific dispute resolution procedures
  • Recording laws that govern whether a parent may record an IEP meeting
  • Voting rights during incarceration (state laws vary significantly)

5 Action Scripts Are Starting Points, Not Legal Strategies

The action scripts and scenario-based guidance in this tool are designed to give families, students, and professionals language to use in IEP meetings. They are starting points for communication — not legal strategies, and not legal documents.

The scripts do not account for the specific facts of your situation, the history of your child's educational record, or the legal posture of a dispute. Using a script does not create any legal rights, preserve any legal claims, or constitute formal notice under IDEA or any other law unless you take additional steps in accordance with proper legal procedures.

If you are involved in or anticipating a formal dispute — including mediation, a state complaint, or due process — consult a licensed special education attorney before taking action.
